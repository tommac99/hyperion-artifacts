The
Boat House
Platform

One owned platform that runs the whole business. Bookings, payments, staff, channels, accounting, safety, franchise — all in one place, all owned by Badedampfer.

Not built from scratch. Not bought as a product. Assembled from the best open-source projects in the world, wrapped in your brand, your data, your control.

The Thesis

Own the valuable core.
Rent only what has real moats.

The whole strategy comes down to a single principle. Every booking, every customer, every transaction, every piece of franchise data — that's the valuable core of Badedampfer, and we own all of it.

The two things we don't try to build are the two with real moats around them: Stripe (because if payment and tax handling is wrong, it's a tax problem, not a feature bug) and Bókun (because access to the big OTAs is gated by a certification process, not by code).

“AI coding assistants have drastically changed the way engineers write code, but they also made finding vulnerabilities in software much easier, especially in publicly-available code. To address this new risk, the codebase of the commercial edition will no longer be publicly available.”

Cal.com — announcing the closed-sourcing of their booking platform, April 2026

This quote shapes how we build. Assembling from open-source is the right strategy. Doing it carelessly is a security risk. So security and reliability are designed in from day one — not added at the end.

Boundary Setting

What We're Explicitly Not Doing

Every "no" here is a decision that makes the platform faster to build and stronger long-term.

01
Not Building From a Blank File
Building a booking platform from zero is years of work. We're not doing that. The booking-and-availability math, the commerce primitives, the notification plumbing — all of these are already solved by mature open-source projects. We assemble them.
02
Not Buying Checkfront
Checkfront would handle most of this — but you'd give up ownership of your customer data, pay a percentage of every booking, and be locked into their roadmap. For a business about to franchise across cities, that's the wrong trade. Once we own the platform, every new city is additive revenue, not additional commission.
03
Not Keeping the Zapier Mesh
The current setup is 18 tools held together by Zapier flows and human action. Every flow is one place something can silently break. The new architecture replaces all of it with one source of truth and direct, owned connections.
04
Not Depending on Mattia's Custom Tools
The Consciousphere financial app and the running-schedule app only Mattia understands — one already abandoned ("not currently maintained"). Critical operations can't live in one person's head. The new platform absorbs everything they do, with proper access controls so anyone on the team can read what they need.

The Open-Source Stack

What Each Project Contributes

For every project on this list, we take a specific, defined piece. We are deliberate about what we don't take, because every line of borrowed code is also a line of responsibility.

Medusa
Commerce backbone · MIT
What We Take

The whole architecture — Medusa's proven way of organising the work. The commerce building blocks: products, carts, orders, discounts, vouchers, refunds. The native multi-location support that maps directly onto franchising.

What We Don't Take

Their default storefront. We build our own customer-facing website on top, so nothing about the look or behaviour is dictated by Medusa.

Cal.diy
Scheduling logic · MIT
What We Take

The math. Timezone handling, daylight-savings transitions, recurring availability windows — the three places teams burn weeks of debugging. We use their logic as the reference, then wire it into our own booking module.

What We Don't Take

The Cal.diy app itself. We don't run their UI or their login system. Borrowing the logic without forking the whole app means we never inherit their security exposure.

Stripe
Payments & tax · Commercial
What We Take

Everything. Card processing, Stripe Connect for franchise splits, Stripe Tax for European VAT, refund handling. This is the one layer where being contractually right matters more than ownership.

What We Don't Take

Nothing — full rental. The trade-off is conscious: Stripe takes a small percentage, and in exchange they're on the hook if anything tax-critical goes wrong.

Bókun
OTA bridge · Free, TripAdvisor-owned
What We Take

The certified connection to every major OTA. Bókun is owned by TripAdvisor, which is why the integration with Viator is native — and why GetYourGuide, Booking.com, Expedia, and Google all work through it.

What We Don't Take

Their booking engine. We use Bókun as a pipe only — our system is the source of truth, Bókun is just how we distribute. (Detail in the channel doc.)

Novu
Notifications · Open-source
What We Take

The whole notification engine. WhatsApp confirmations to customers, shift alerts to crew, cancellation messages, review-request emails — all flow through one system instead of fifteen Zapier chains.

What We Don't Take

Their hosted version. We self-host it so notification data never leaves your control.

Documenso
Digital signatures · AGPL
What We Take

The signing flow. Customers sign waivers digitally before stepping on a boat. Every signature is timestamped, legally valid in Germany, stored against the booking.

What We Don't Take

The source code itself. Documenso uses a license (AGPL) that requires anyone who modifies it to share their changes publicly. We use it only through its API — we never modify the source — so this restriction never applies to us.

Twenty
CRM · AGPL
What We Take

The CRM. Franchise partners, B2B customers (hotels, event planners, corporate gift buyers), pipeline tracking — managed in one place. Same API-only integration approach as Documenso.

What We Don't Take

Direct integration into the source code. Twenty connects to The Boat House via API only — so customer data and CRM data stay separate, with clear boundaries.

Sentry
Error tracking · Commercial / OSS
What We Take

Real-time error tracking. The moment anything breaks in production — a failed payment, a stuck booking, a broken integration — we know about it before a customer reports it.

What We Don't Take

Nothing — pure observability service. Stays out of the data path, in only when something is wrong.

Built To Last

Security & Reliability,
by Design

The Cal.com quote at the top of this document is the reason this section exists. Assembling from open-source is the right strategy — but only if it's done with security and reliability designed in from day one, not added later as a panic response.

01
Franchise Data Stays in Its Own Lane

Each city's bookings, customers, and revenue are isolated from every other city's. Tested explicitly on every release — not assumed.

02
No Modified Open-Source

Anything with a strict open-source license (Documenso, Twenty) is used via API only. We never modify the source — so we never inherit obligations to publish our code.

03
Always-On Error Tracking

Every failure in production fires an alert the moment it happens. We hear about problems before customers do.

04
No Hardcoded Secrets

Every key, password, and API token lives in a secrets manager — not in the code. Rotation is one command, not a code change.

05
Audit Trail on Everything

Every booking, payment, refund, and staff action is logged with who, when, and what. If anything ever needs to be reconstructed, it can be.

06
The Concurrency Test

The classic booking-system bug: two customers grab the same last slot at the same instant. The booking module is built and tested to prevent this from day one.

What This Requires

A real build needs a real builder.

The honest version: this is not a weekend project, and it's not a part-time side gig. "Assemble from open-source" is faster and far safer than building from scratch, but a custom booking module on Medusa's architecture, wired to Stripe Connect, plugged into Bókun, with a clean franchise-ready UI and German accounting integration — that's a real four-to-five months of focused engineering work.

That work needs one full-time owner-builder, not three part-time contractors. Spread across multiple people it loses coherence; spread across nights and weekends it never finishes. The platform IS the company's IP — it deserves a single mind with full context, building it from end to end.

And the build doesn't end at launch. The person building it becomes the platform's maintainer — running migrations as new channels are added, handling Sentry alerts, writing the next franchise onboarding flow when Vienna goes live. This is a long-term role, not a project.

The right structure reflects that: not a fixed project fee, but a proper engagement — equity, employment, or a hybrid that aligns the builder with the business they're building. The platform is too important to the company's future to be built on a transactional basis.

02

Hosting Decision

Medusa can be self-hosted (full ownership, around €50-100/month infrastructure) or run on Medusa Cloud (managed, faster to start, monthly fee). Self-hosting is the right long-term call for a franchise business. Cloud removes early friction. Pick which matters more right now.

Decide
03

Stripe Account Setup

Badedampfer needs a verified Stripe business account with VAT number and bank account, plus a Stripe Connect platform account for franchise splits. Verification takes one to five working days and cannot be rushed. Start in parallel with Phase 1.

Start Now
04

Bókun Account Creation

Create the Bókun supplier account and begin listing products (boats, time-slots, pricing). OTA certification clocks (especially GetYourGuide's) start from account creation date. Starting in Week 1 saves four to six weeks later.

Start Now
05

JSMD Supplier Registration

Jochen Schweizer and mydays share the same JSMD Group supplier portal. Approval can take four to eight weeks. Register as early as possible so Phase 6 isn't blocked waiting on their approval while everything else is live.

Start Now

Product Inventory Definition

How many boats? What are the distinct products — standard rental, premium, group session? What are the time-slot windows, session durations, capacity per slot? This is the data model that everything else builds on. Needs Jannis's input early.

Scope Decision
08

Loop in the Badedampfer accountant now. Confirm DATEV is the target format (it almost certainly is — it's the German standard). Establish the chart of accounts the platform should map to. Plan the first reconciliation handoff.
